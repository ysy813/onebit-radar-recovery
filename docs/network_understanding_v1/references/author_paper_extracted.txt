A Convolutional De-Quantization Network for
Harmonics Suppression in One-Bit SAR Imaging
Cuiqi Si , Bo Zhao , Member, IEEE, Lei Huang , Senior Member, IEEE, and Shiqi Liu
Abstract— One-bit synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imaging,
which collects echoes into one-bit quantized samples, is a highly
promising technique for the faster SAR images acquisition and
simplified implementations in numerous applications. Harmonics,
accompanied with one-bit sampling operation, are one of the
fundamental factors that trigger severe aliasing artifacts problem
in frequency domain. For one-bit SAR imaging, rather than
sampling the echoes with extremely high rate to alleviate the
effects of harmonics, a convolutional de-quantization network,
termed as CDQOB-net, is proposed to establish de-aliasing in a
transformed imagery domain. The proposed method starts with a
fresh interpretation on the regular range-Doppler (RD) imaging,
which is also referred to as the 2-D transformed domain of SAR
echo. Then, in the light of deep learning framework for image
restoration, an alternative method designed in the perspective
of harmonics is specifically analyzed, wherein the degradation
caused by harmonics in transformed domain is sufficiently
estimated. Consequently, as a data-driven method, our two-stage
CDQOB-net is proposed and trained to handle aliasing harmonics
and multiple distortions, which can significantly improve the
quality of one-bit SAR imaging. The proposed method is easy to
implement and computationally efficient. Extensive experiments
on simulated and real SAR data also demonstrate its validation
and effectiveness.
Index Terms— Deep neural networks, Fourier image restora-
tion, harmonic suppression, one-bit sampling, synthetic aperture
radar (SAR) imaging.

I. INTRODUCTION
S
YTHETIC aperture radar (SAR) has been applied in
various remote sensing applications [1], particularly it can
illuminate the surveillance regions with high resolution in all
time of day and under severe weather conditions [2]. Due to
these merits, SAR image formation [3], [4] has become an
important research topic, and how to deduce the geometry
characteristics of ground patches from the observed raw SAR
data [5] is the most essential task in imaging operation. As a
wideband signal is usually transmitted for SAR imaging to
guarantee high resolution, a high sampling rate is required
for undistorted echo collection, which also leads to a huge
volume of data. Even though the de-chirping technology has
been adopted to reduce the sampling rate [6], the remained
data is still excessive for SAR image formation. To generate
SAR images from raw data, resources including storage,
transmission, and computing power are desperately demanded,
which not only slows down the whole processing chain, but
also brings great burden to hardware. Motivated by faster
and cheaper SAR imaging, one-bit quantization technology
has been utilized. By mapping the multivalued raw data into
signed sequences {+1, −1} [7], [8], [9], an extremely simple
and high efficient imaging structure are implemented for SAR
echo processing [10], and the reduced data amounts alleviate
the storage and transmission burdens as well.
One-bit quantization, which performs many-to-one mapping
on the raw data, is regarded as a complicated nonlinear
process. As a result, the compressed one-bit raw SAR echoes
pose challenges to information recovery, say, SAR imaging
algorithm. For one-bit signal processing, existing methods can

be generally classified into two categories, i.e., prior-based
methods and redundant sampling strategy-based methods. The
prior-based methods usually make a sparsity assumption on the
signal, and employee methodologies such as statistical signal
processing [11], [12], [13], [14], matrix sensing [15], [16], and
compressive sensing (CS) [17], [18] scheme to solve the one-
bit imaging as an optimization problem. As the information
to be recovered are constrained by the sparse prior, the one-
bit data, even suffers significant degradation compared to the
multibit data, is still redundant for the sparse information.
The prior-based methods can therefore work effectively. How-
ever, in the SAR imaging application, the sparsity-constrained
algorithms focus on several strong scatterers and ignore the
other weak targets in the SAR scenario, resulting in too brief
images with absence of important properties. Moreover, these
algorithms usually convert the 2-D backscattering coefficient
matrix into 1-D vector, which destructs the internal spatial
structures adversely.
When the sparsity assumption is not satisfied, the redundant
sampling strategy is adopted [7], [10], [19]. By using an
over-sampling rate that is much greater than the Nyquist
rate, the number of one-bit samples is increased, while an
extra space is extended in the frequency domain. Superfluous
frequency-domain space is able to accommodate the high-
order harmonics caused by sudden flips between one-bit data.
In this way, the spectrum aliasing is avoided for high-order
harmonics, making them suppressible through a matched filter,
and thus improves the quality of one-bit signal processing for
a more common case. In general, both categories of methods
require a superfluity of samples although the quantization level
has been decreased into one-bit, and suffer from hand-designed
hyperparameters that hinder the one-bit signal processing from
high efficiency.
When the one-bit data is collected at a regular Nyquist rate
without additional prior involved, a great challenge is posed for
the existing methods. From the perspective of CS, non-sparse
assumption makes the equations in the optimization problem
under-determined. In the view of spectrum analysis, the high-
order harmonics, whose bandwidths have been multiplied in
one-bit quantization and are much greater than the original sig-
nal, will alias seriously when collected at the regular Nyquist
rate being slightly larger than the signal bandwidth. Therefore,
how to recover the information deep-seated in the one-bit data
is an essential problem for one-bit signal processing to achieve
better performance at lower data volume.
As an alternative, deep learning-based methods [20] have
attracted considerable attentions due to their favorable recon-
struction or recovery performances. Very recently, deep neural
architectures are combined with radar signal processing to
solve signal recovery issues, termed as “deep unfolding” [21]
architectures. In these models, researchers designed deep con-
volutional networks [22] to unfold the iterative procedures of
approximation in model-based optimization problem, such as
alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)-net [23]
and deep-fixed-point continuation (FPC) [24]. It avoids manu-
factured parameters and offers high reconstruction accuracy
in specific applications, such as direction-of-arrival (DOA)
estimation.
In this work, with the first attempt to tackle the one-bit
radar imaging problem through the deep learning framework,
we investigate the following two questions as: 1) Can we train
a deep model to handle multiple variant degradation in one-bit
measurement and 2) Is it possible to directly use 2-D echo data
to train the model with high practicability? Instead of repre-
senting multiple harmonics caused by one-bit quantization as
plain noises, it is motivated to describe harmonics as strongly
signal-dependent and adverse degradation of spatial variant.
In addition, in order to preserve spatial-structured correlations
in SAR imaging, we directly encode one-bit echoes into 2-D
Fourier domains, and also offer a convincing explanation for
aliased harmonics in the transformed domain. To eliminate
these serious impacts and to improve the efficiency for one-
bit SAR imaging, a convolutional de-quantization network
(CDQOB-net) framework is proposed and trained to pinpoint
the root of aliased artifacts, and estimate the harmonic kernel
separately from system noises. Then, with a step-by-step
processing, the one-bit SAR imaging task can be accomplished
with high quality.
The main contributions are summarized as follows.
1) A simple yet effective signal formulation model is
proposed for one-bit SAR imaging. The proposed signal
model goes beyond the widely used 2-D SAR data disen-
tangled assumption and works for instant range-Doppler
(RD) images in transformed domain, thus making a
substantial preprocess step toward developing a practical
convolutional neural networks (CNNs)-based one-bit
recovery resolver in real-world applications.
2) A novel module is developed to represent the high-
ordered harmonic degradation, which depends exclu-
sively on the input baseband signal and are distorted
by aliasing impact. Therefore, with this harmonic esti-
mation module, a CDQOB-net is proposed and learned
for one-bit SAR imaging.
3) To quantify the plausibility of our proposed scheme,
simulated and real-measured one-bit SAR imagery
dataset are established. We show that the proposed
model learned from simulation and real data not only
can perform competitive results against several represen-
tative one-bit SAR imaging methods, but also produce
more plausible results for down-streamed imaging tasks,
such as target recognition in SAR interpretation [25].
The rest of this work is organized as follows. Section II
briefly introduces the proposed signal model. Then, Section III
proposes deep learning-based framework for one-bit SAR
imaging in detail. In Section IV, extensive experiments are
conducted to validate the effectiveness and demonstrate the
superiority of the proposed model. Finally, Section V con-
cludes the whole frameworks.

We first elaborate the one-bit SAR imaging scheme in

Then, an analysis of harmonic degradation in transformed

To solve the above equations, instead of directly operating
Fourier coding on one-bit matrix B to approximate ˆZRD,
an alternative way is provided with the intuition that the
scattering characteristics of the targets are reflected as the
frequency properties in Bdc. With this insight, we turn to
analyze one-bit quantized echoes which are projected into the
2-D frequency spectrum domain. In the transformed domain,
besides, one-bit recovery can be considered as a specific
image restoration problem. In particular, according to (15),
we define F(B) as 2-D Fourier transformations for B, which
can be considered as F(·) = Fτ(·)Fη. It is intuitive to
declare that now we are making efforts to recover F(Bdc)
from F(B).
Therefore, in the imagery domain, the transformed one-
bit quantization error is considered as two individual parts:
the noise-like behaved high-order harmonics and the inter-
modulation components which have strongly correlative
information with baseband signal. It is known that former
errors exhibit as Gaussian-like distribution in system, and
a typical denoising-based framework would work well in
a strictly distributed condition. On the other side, along
with the sampling representation reduced to binary form, the
high-ordered harmonics will constantly emerge. Due to the
under-sampling rate for one-bit form, the aliased impacts of
multiple harmonics are distortedly placed and discontinuously
correlated. Correspondingly, relied on the typical restoration
framework, the RD image acquired from the one-bit data, say
F(B), would be formulated as
F(B) = H ◦F(Bdc) + N0.
(18)
In this setting, F(B) is the observed quantization image,
and F(Bdc) is the ideal “clean” image to be recovered in
transformed domain. H is a nonlinear operator that corresponds
to the responses of Fourier transformation in one-bit quanti-
zation, ◦is the Hadamard product and N0 accounts for the
former error, including noise under Fourier imaging operator
and harmonics with infinite orders, which can be commonly
assumed as i.i.d. Gaussian noise with standard deviation σ.

V. CONCLUDING REMARKS
In this article, CDQOB-net is proposed to estimate and
suppress multiple harmonics in Fourier imagery domain. For
one-bit SAR imaging subsequently, this two-step module is
trained to provide a high image quality via a data-driven
restoration method. Rather than utilizing extra samples to
absorb the impacts caused by nonlinear signum function, the
proposed CDQOB-net provides an alternative framework that
learns the nonlinear degradation and irreversible spectrum
aliasing effects in transformed domain. Based on specific-
defined decoupling RD images, a preliminary statement of
instantaneous SAR imaging is given. Beyond that, RVB-oSAR
dataset is specifically established for further exploiting the
new insights of one-bit signal processing. The results on
simulations and real-measured SAR data have confirmed that
the proposed network is faster implemented and offers a com-
petitive performances compared with other existing methods.
